# definir-objetivo

Goal: two to four concrete **job families** they could apply to next month, each with real titles, a seniority level backed by evidence, and the sectors where they exist. It ends in their decision, never yours.

## Steps

1. **Start from what exists.** If `profile/PROFILE.md` is built, read it. If not, ask for their CV first (or run the first steps of `armar-perfil`).
2. **Ask, one question at a time** (wait for each answer, stop when you have enough):
   - Of everything they did, what they enjoyed most and what they would never do again.
   - What they are proud of (a result, a project, something people come to them for).
   - What matters most now: money, learning, stability, flexibility, prestige, purpose. Ask them to rank the top three.
   - Constraints: city, travel, hours, languages, starting date.
   - Any role or company they already have in mind, even vaguely.
3. **Propose 2 to 4 job families.** For each one, in a few lines:
   - Typical titles (the words that appear in postings, so they work as search terms).
   - Why it fits them, citing their evidence (a job, a result, a skill from PROFILE).
   - Realistic seniority, with the reason. Do not inflate and do not sell them short.
   - The main gap and how they would cover it in an interview.
   - Sectors and example companies in their country.
   If you check the market (number of postings, titles in use), do it with a quick search in the built-in browser and say it is a snapshot of today. Never quote salaries from memory (see CLAUDE.md, Salary data).
4. **Let them choose** and order the families. Record in `profile/preferences.md`: target roles in order, seniority, sectors, and what they ranked as most important. Keep their words.
5. **Hand off**: the families become the search terms of `armar-perfil` step 6b and, if they want, one CV per family (`cv/variantes/<familia>-es.md`, fixed-CV mode).

## Rules

- Never assume a field, sector or ambition. Ask.
- A change of field is fine: say plainly which evidence carries over and what is missing.
- Short messages, their register, no dashes in Spanish.
